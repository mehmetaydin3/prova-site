# Prova value proposition

Source of truth: the founder brief. Product claims below trace to its product facts and sticking points. Market context comes only from its supplied insight. Benefits describe the product’s intended value; retention and commercial outcomes remain hypotheses to prove.

## 1. Core statement

Prova keeps great teaching on the music, so students carry the lesson into every practice.

## 2. Tagline options

- Great teaching stays with the music.
- The lesson comes home with you.
- A mark that carries the lesson.
- Keep the lesson on the stand.
- Carry the teaching. Make it yours.

Lead with **“Great teaching stays with the music.”** Use the others as supporting expressions of the same promise.

## 3. Positioning statement

For independent music teachers and their students who need lesson guidance to carry into daily practice, Prova is an iPhone and iPad practice app that keeps teacher feedback on the student’s score. Unlike solo practice apps or sprawling studio platforms, Prova centers the assign → practice → record → listen → mark up loop, with separate student and teacher annotation layers on the same music.

## 4. Three pillars

### Leave a thought that stays

Mark the phrase where your guidance belongs.  
Your student finds it on the score when they sit down to practice again.

### Carry the lesson. Make it yours.

Keep your own marks alongside your teacher’s, with Mine, Teacher or Both.  
Open Today with a clear next step, then bring your own thinking to the music.

### Hear what carries forward

Students record a take and choose what to share privately with their teacher.  
Listen, see their practice time, and leave the next thought on the score.

## 5. Audience versions

### Teachers

The lesson ends. Your teaching stays with the music. In Prova, mark the phrase you want your student to hear differently. They find your guidance on their score when they practice at home. Listen to the take they share, then leave the next thought where it belongs: beside the notes.

- **Let your guidance travel home.** Your marks stay on the score, in a separate teacher layer students can return to.
- **Give them a place to begin.** Your practice steps, daily target and materials are ready in Today.
- **Hear what they carry forward.** Shared takes and tracked practice time help you shape the next lesson.

### Students

Keep your teacher’s guidance on your music, beside your own marks. Open Today, find your next step, and practice with everything on the stand. Bring pieces you love along, too.

### Parents

Your child sees their teacher’s guidance on the score, helping them know what to practice next. Takes stay private unless shared with their teacher. Parents never pay to use Prova.

## 6. Investor narrative

### The lesson needs somewhere to continue

The teacher marks a phrase. The student opens that same score at home and sees the guidance beside the notes, alongside their own marks. That is Prova’s central moment: feedback remains present where practice happens.

Between lessons, teachers have limited visibility into the work. A text or notebook separates feedback from the music it concerns. Students need a clear place to begin; teachers need to hear what happened and leave guidance that carries forward.

### Focus is the category lesson

The founder brief describes a split between polished solo practice apps and broad teacher platforms. Solo tools leave the teacher outside the loop. Broad platforms spread their attention across studio administration, social features and game economies. Prova’s thesis is that the essential practice relationship deserves the product’s full attention.

### Why this loop can endure

Prova connects assigning, practicing, recording, listening and marking up. Today makes the next action clear. The score, metronome, tuner and recorder stay together on the stand. Students share takes privately; teachers reply with comments and marks on the score. Mine, Teacher and Both views preserve student ownership while keeping guidance accessible. Students can also bring their own music, visible read-only to a linked teacher with that access disclosed.

The potential score-markup data moat is the useful record created through this relationship. Each week adds marked scores, takes and practice history to the teacher’s studio. A student has reasons to return to that guidance; a teacher has reasons to preserve that context. Replacing an increasingly valuable record creates switching friction. Prova is free during beta. When pricing arrives, teachers pay; students and parents never do. Teachers bring their studios, while students’ daily practice and shared takes make the teacher’s subscription useful. Distribution and retention therefore meet in the same relationship: the teacher introduces the tool, and the practice loop gives both musicians a reason to stay. ## 8. Objection answers

### “Why not just text videos?”

Prova keeps your teacher’s marks on the score, beside the phrase they mean. Return to the guidance while you practice, then record and privately share a take.

### “Why not a free practice timer app?”

Time tells you how long you practiced. Prova also keeps your teacher’s guidance on the music and connects your next take to their next response.

### “Is my child’s data safe?”

Takes stay private unless shared with the teacher. A linked teacher can see student-added pieces read-only, and the app tells the student. TODO(founder): provide the privacy policy, retention and deletion details, and verified security practices for a fuller answer.

### “What happened to Tonara, and why won’t that happen to you?” — investor only

Tonara shut down; the brief does not establish why, and no business can promise immunity. Prova’s thesis is a focused practice-and-feedback loop, a teacher-paid model after beta, and no game economy; durability must be demonstrated through teacher retention and recurring studio use.

### Selected: Great teaching stays with the music.

It gives the product’s most distinctive act a human meaning: a teacher’s thought is still there when a student needs it. Teachers hear respect for their craft. Students hear support they can carry into their own playing. Parents hear continuity. Investors can connect that recurring usefulness to the growing record of marked scores, takes and practice history.

**“Stays” is the central idea.** It makes the reason to return tangible: open the music and the guidance is there. The separate layers preserve the student’s ownership. The shared take brings the teacher back into the loop. Together, these make an enduring promise without claiming proven retention or musical outcomes.

The creative rule is simple: **follow the same mark.** Begin with the teacher’s intention, move close enough to read the mark on the phrase, then pull back to the student practicing with it in view. “Feedback that lives on the music” explains the mechanism; “the week between lessons” supplies the setting. Neither needs to compete with the lead promise.
